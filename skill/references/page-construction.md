# Page construction and product imagery

## Narrative

A landing page should answer, in order appropriate to the product:
1. What is this and who is it for?
2. Why should I care now?
3. What does it actually do?
4. Can I trust it?
5. How does it fit my work?
6. What should I do next?

The hero need not answer every question. A type-led hero often performs better visually when one strong product proof follows at a legible size.

## Section roles

Assign each section one dominant role: promise, orientation, proof, explanation, comparison, trust, objection handling or action. If a section has several roles, split it or establish a clear primary and secondary hierarchy.

Alternate density. A dense interface proof should not be followed immediately by another equally dense grid. Use a type-led pause, principle row, quote, metric or negative space to reset attention.

## Image roles and ratios

- Product overview: 16:9, 16:10 or ultrawide.
- Focused workflow: 3:2 or 4:3.
- Capability comparison: 1:1 or 5:4 in a local grid.
- Device/editorial interruption: 4:5, 5:6 or natural portrait.
- Environment/divider: 2:1 or wider when legibility survives.

Use a dominant ratio, a supporting ratio and an occasional interruption. Do not vary ratios randomly.

Every image must prove, explain or set an intentional editorial mood. Name the role before creating the asset.

## Crop method

Preserve orientation, action and consequence. Remove distant chrome, inactive tabs, empty canvas and repeated navigation first. A crop should make the claim legible at the final rendered size.

On mobile choose re-crop, reorder, progressive disclosure or substitution. Semantic equivalence matters more than geometric sameness.

## Frames

Use one restrained outer surface, one hairline and one inner radius. Add browser or device chrome only when that context helps understanding. Keep shadows soft and limited. Never stack a fake coded product scene with finished proof of the same idea.

## Bento

Use bento when multiple capabilities must be compared simultaneously and benefit from unequal emphasis. Limit it to one local cluster, usually three to six cells. Vary internal composition based on content; do not place the same title/body/icon template in every cell. Return to a simpler section after the cluster.

## Patterns by category

- SaaS: clear promise, overview proof, outcome/features, integrations/trust, action.
- Developer tool: concrete code or operational proof, architecture/workflow, reliability, docs path.
- Desktop app: calm download hero, large app image, platform details, shortcuts/workflows.
- Editorial/course: strong type contrast, article/lesson rhythm, annotated examples, author credibility.
- Event: cinematic promise, date/location certainty, program, speakers/social proof, conversion bar.
- Component library: live components, states, copy/install path, accessibility and coverage.
- Docs: navigation/search proof, code/content split, try-it surface, clear entry point.

## Dataset finding

The 245-site dark.design audit found grid signals on 61.1% of 211 inspectable listings, motion on 72.0%, responsive images on 57.3%, video on 43.6%, frames on 30.3%, card naming on 22.7% and explicit bento/masonry naming on 2.4%. Declared image ratios were 48.4% square-ish, 26.1% landscape, 14.0% ultrawide and 11.5% portrait. Treat these as structural indicators with known DOM-detection limits, not universal rules.
